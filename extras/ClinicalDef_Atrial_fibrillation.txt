# Atrial Fibrillation

## Clinical Case Definition
* Atrial fibrillation is the most common cardiac arrhythmia, characterized by disorganized, chaotic electrical signals that override the sinus node.
* **Classification:**
  * **Paroxysmal:** Lasting < 7 days.
  * **Persistent:** Lasting > 7 days.
  * **Long-standing persistent:** Lasting > 1 year.
  * **Permanent AF:** Accepted condition with no further rhythm control attempt.

## Clinical Presentation and Symptoms
* Symptoms include palpitations, chest pain, dizziness, fatigue, lightheadedness, reduced ability to exercise, shortness of breath, and weakness.
* Can also present with hemodynamic changes.

## Causes and Risk Factors
* **Age:** The most important risk factor.
* **Sex:** Risk is greater in males than females.
* **Comorbidities:** Hypertension, cardiovascular and structural heart disease, diabetes mellitus (DM), obesity, sleep apnea.
* **Lifestyle/other:** Alcohol, smoking, family history, hyperthyroidism, lung disease.

## Diagnostic Evaluation
* Physical examination.
* Electrocardiogram (ECG or EKG).
* Holter monitor.
* Echocardiogram.
* Stress tests.
* Blood tests.

## Treatment and Management
* Treating underlying diseases and modifying lifestyle to reduce or eliminate risk factors.
* **Pharmacological:**
  * **Managing symptoms (rate control):** Beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin.
  * **Managing symptoms (rhythm control):** Amiodarone, dofetilide, dronedarone, flecainide, propafenone, sotalol.
  * **Stroke prevention (blood thinners/anticoagulants):** The CHA2DS2-VASc score (or similar clinical risk calculator) must be used to determine if a patient's stroke risk warrants the bleeding risks of anticoagulation therapy. Options include DOACs/FXa inhibitors (apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban, edoxaban) or warfarin.
* **MedTech:**
  * **Acute rhythm restoration:** Electrical or pharmacologic cardioversion.
  * **Durable rhythm control:** Catheter ablation.
  * **Stroke prevention:** Left atrium appendage occlusion (LAAO).

## Prognosis
* Results in significant morbidity: increased risk of stroke (5-fold), heart failure, dementia and cognitive impairment, and chronic fatigue.
* Associated with an increased risk of mortality.
